# Autonomous Collective Intelligence — Engineering Program

**Status:** draft engineering program recording Principal direction
**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Governing basis:** [Founding Mandate](FOUNDING-MANDATE.md) and explicit
Principal direction
**Research basis:** [Autonomous Collective Agent
Systems](research/autonomous-collective-agent-systems.md)

## Purpose

Rossovia is being built toward a self-sensing, self-adaptive, self-organizing
collective intelligence composed of Agents.

This is the final **engineering operating form** in service of the Founding
Mandate, not the project's final social purpose. Autonomy is valuable because
it can make productive AI capability more broadly accessible, economical,
inspectable, replaceable, and useful without turning people into workflow
operators. A system that runs longer while becoming more opaque, expensive,
provider-dependent, extractive, or difficult for practitioners to retain and
change has moved away from Rossovia's purpose.

The operating form is not a task manager with Agent workers, a larger
orchestration workflow, or a supervised control room. It is an engineering
system that can receive naturally expressed human intent, remain engaged with
reality over time, organize the work needed to change that reality, observe the
result, correct itself, and continue without a person operating every task and
handoff.

Semi-autonomous operation is a necessary transition because present Agents and
the present system cannot yet close that whole relation reliably. It is not the
destination. Workbench supervision, Principal decision queues, manual task
creation, and fixed authorization surfaces are scaffolding for missing
capability. They must not become the architecture's reason for existing.

## Governance boundary

The Principle Sequence remains Rossovia's only semantic root. The Founding
Mandate supplies the social purpose this program serves. This document records
project-specific engineering direction and tests; it cannot add or reinterpret
a P-ID, manufacture consent, enlarge runtime authority, accept a result, or
authorize an autonomous transition.

Until an accepted design change and representative operating evidence say
otherwise, the current supervised decisions remain in force. The commitments
below are revisable through evidence and accepted project design. Descriptions
of future system behavior are capability targets, not permissions for today's
runtime.

## The final operating relation

The intended human-system relation is:

```text
human purpose, values, constraints, and lived response
                         ⇅
                 natural interaction
                         ⇅
     shared intent, cognition, and collective direction
                         ⇅
        nested, adaptive loops of temporary Agents
                         ⇅
             tools, projects, worktrees, and effects
                         ⇅
                reality and practice evidence
```

The person should not need to translate an intention into an Agent topology,
queue, workflow, prompt chain, or sequence of approvals. The system should not
hide uncertainty behind confident activity. Natural interaction lets either
side contribute at the level where it has something decision-changing to add:
the person supplies meaning, correction, preference, and judgment about real
outcomes; the collective system supplies sustained operational intelligence.

Fully autonomous operation means that, within an explicitly entrusted purpose
and accepted authority, resource, effect, and data envelope under actual
operating conditions, the system can keep the productive loop closed:

```text
sense → understand → form → act → observe → verify → adapt → continue
  ↑                                                        │
  └──────────────── retained practice state ───────────────┘
```

This does not mean perfect prediction, unlimited action, or independence from
people and the world. It means freedom from stepwise human operation. A person
may still change the purpose, reject an outcome, introduce new knowledge, or
make a judgment that cannot be derived from system evidence. These are natural
parts of collective intelligence, not a standing requirement that the person
administer the machine.

## The system lives through loops

The Loop is the system's basic living relation. It is not a prescribed sequence
of prompt stages. A Loop exists when:

- something in reality is being brought toward an intended condition;
- the current condition can be observed well enough to choose an action;
- an Agent or mechanism can act on that condition;
- the effect is observed again;
- the observation can change what happens next; and
- enough state survives for the work to continue after a turn, failure,
  process exit, model change, or Agent replacement.

A loop that only produces messages is not closed. A retry that sees no new
evidence is not learning. A dashboard without a response path is not
self-sensing. A verifier whose result cannot alter later work is not feedback.

Loops nest because engineering problems exist at different scopes and time
horizons. A local loop may repair one action, a task loop may change a plan, a
project loop may reorganize dependencies and resources, and a collective loop
may revise a method or the system's understanding of the problem. These are
illustrative horizons, not permanent services or departments.

The wider loop should not continuously micromanage the narrower one. It acts
when local evidence exposes a contradiction that the local loop cannot resolve:
repeated failure, a broken dependency, exhausted resources, a failed method,
conflicting goals, or a changed environment. Local loops preserve speed and
specificity; wider loops preserve direction and the ability to reorganize.

## Running longer is a source of capability

Rossovia adopts a falsifiable working hypothesis: some Agent tasks improve not
because their visible workflow becomes more elaborate, but because useful
engagement with the object continues for longer. More time can permit another
search, tool call, experiment, comparison, repair, or reflection. Existing
[evidence supports this in bounded reasoning and parallel research
settings](research/autonomous-collective-agent-systems.md#bounded-evidence-for-spending-more-time-and-compute);
it does not establish a general law for long-running autonomous work.

The project must test when the hypothesis holds. Time and tokens are only fuel.
They may compound into capability when each additional interval retains the
preceding state, encounters the environment, and has a chance to revise the
next move. Rossovia should optimize for **useful continuation**, not short
answers, turn completion, Agent utilization, or activity volume.

The system may continue while:

- new observations are arriving;
- the observations can still change a decision;
- actions are making measurable progress or reducing relevant uncertainty;
- a different method, Agent, tool, or organization remains plausible; or
- recovery is restoring a broken but still valuable line of work.

It should change level, reorganize, or stop when motion becomes repetition,
feedback no longer discriminates among actions, the intended outcome has been
reached, or the purpose itself must be reconsidered.

## The collective is the unit of intelligence

An Agent is a replaceable participant, not the enduring subject of the system.
It may sense, reason, act, verify, coordinate, or converse for one period of
work. Its identity does not own the mission, facts, or organization.

A Work Cell is the smallest isolated practice unit: a prepared encounter
between one bounded intent, an Agent or mechanical actor, a workspace, tools,
and observable evidence. It permits local action and local failure without
making one context or process the system's mind.

A Swarm is a temporary topology of differentiated Cells and Agents selected
because the work benefits from parallel exploration, specialization,
independent evidence, or distributed response. It is not a synonym for
multiple calls. Sequential work may require one continuing Agent; strongly
parallel work may require many; mixed work may reorganize between those forms.

For this program, collective intelligence will be accepted only when the whole
repeatedly performs better because its participants:

- share the facts and intent required for reconstruction without sharing one
  polluted global context;
- divide work along real dependency and evidence boundaries;
- expose different failure modes rather than duplicate one correlated answer;
- return contributions to a loop capable of synthesis and correction;
- change their organization when the present structure stops serving the
  objective; and
- retain useful methods and evidence after individual Agents disappear.

Agent count, simultaneous activity, consensus, hierarchy, token use, and visual
complexity do not establish collective intelligence.

## Self-sensing, self-adaptation, and self-organization

These terms name observable system abilities.

### Self-sensing

The system can reconstruct what it is trying to do, what is actually happening,
which Agents and resources are active, what changed, what is blocked or
uncertain, and which evidence supports that reading. It senses external
outcomes as well as internal activity. Agent self-description is one signal,
not the system state.

### Self-adaptation

Observed outcomes change later behavior. The system can alter a plan, method,
model, tool, budget allocation, concurrency, verification path, or recovery
strategy because evidence shows that the previous choice no longer fits. A
stored lesson that never changes a later decision is archive, not adaptation.

### Self-organization

The system can form, reshape, combine, and dissolve working relations in
response to the current contradiction. It does not require a person to specify
the Agent chart for every goal. Self-organization may use centralized,
distributed, hierarchical, independent, or hybrid coordination as the work
requires; it is not committed to one topology as an ideology.

### Collective learning

Practice changes not only one run but the collective's later capacity. Evidence
may revise a local task, an organizational pattern, a Skill, an accepted
design, or—through its own human-governed path—a principle interpretation or
source. No single Agent silently rewrites the system's governing meaning.

## Natural interaction

Natural interaction is not a chat window placed in front of a task system. It
is a relationship in which a person can speak from their own problem and the
system can respond at the level of intent.

The normal interaction should be:

- express an outcome, concern, constraint, preference, or correction;
- perceive how the collective currently understands and pursues it;
- receive results, material uncertainty, and meaningful changes;
- refine or redirect the same living object without rebuilding its task tree;
  and
- leave the system to continue when no human contribution is presently needed.

In the final operating form, the system should ask when a missing human
judgment would materially change the outcome and cannot be resolved inside the
explicitly accepted envelope. Within that envelope, it should learn when to
preserve momentum through reversible work, uncertainty reduction, narrower
effects, dependency rearrangement, or waiting without freezing unrelated work.
This is a target capability, not current authority. Existing intent, evidence,
or prior correction may guide operational choices inside the envelope; none
may be used to infer consent, authority enlargement, data disclosure, result
acceptance, publication, merge, or an irreversible external commitment.

Feedback should attach to the thing being corrected and become perceptible in
later behavior. The person should not have to understand internal Agent roles
or protocol names to say that a result is wrong, a direction is unwanted, or a
constraint has changed.

## Workbench is a perceptual surface

Workbench is the present shell through which a Principal sees multiple
projects, worktrees, tasks, Agents, effects, and decisions. Its current
task-oriented and supervised form is useful because the underlying system is
not yet sufficiently self-sensing or autonomous.

The final surface should feel less like operating a queue and more like
perceiving a living collective. A field of points that brighten, dim, cluster,
separate, and exchange signals may express this well if every visual state
projects a real operational relation:

- brightness corresponds to current useful activity or attention;
- grouping corresponds to a temporary formation around shared work;
- motion corresponds to a real organizational transition;
- connections correspond to current dependencies or evidence exchange;
- disturbance corresponds to blocked, conflicting, degraded, or recovering
  state; and
- quiet corresponds honestly to waiting, completion, or absence of useful
  work.

This field is one possible expression, not a required implementation. Task
lists, timelines, project views, conversation, and generated controls may
coexist as alternate projections. None should become the backend domain model.
The underlying system remains designed for Agents and loops; the interface
helps people perceive, converse with, and occasionally redirect that system.

## Project-specific engineering commitments

These commitments express the current program and remain revisable through
practice evidence and accepted design change.

1. **Reality closes the loop.** An Agent report, event, trace, or UI state is a
   claim until connected to the relevant external or project evidence.
2. **Intent and state outlive Agents.** A process, model, session, role, or
   provider can be replaced without erasing the purpose, current contradiction,
   accepted facts, open effects, or recovery point.
3. **Recovery cannot depend on perfect event delivery.** The system must be
   able to reconstruct a valid next action from authoritative intent and
   observed state after interruption, while retaining the causal history needed
   to understand effects, attribution, and unresolved work.
4. **Prefer locally intelligible loops.** Scope a loop narrowly enough that its
   feedback and recovery remain distinguishable when that improves operation.
   Wider coordination exists where local loops cannot preserve a shared
   constraint or outcome; the runtime owner determines the concrete topology.
5. **Cells isolate effects; facts remain shareable.** Local context, failure,
   and resource consumption should not contaminate the collective. Relevant
   evidence must still return to shared cognition.
6. **Organization follows the work.** Permanent Agent departments, universal
   councils, and mandatory pipelines are suspect unless representative
   operation shows that their continuity improves the outcome.
7. **Verification enables continuation.** It provides a discriminating signal
   for the next move. It must not grow into approval ceremony disconnected from
   an observed failure consequence.
8. **Resource pressure is part of feedback.** The system must expose when
   activity is exhausting useful capacity. The owning runtime selects
   admission, concurrency, backpressure, or another response only where the
   observed failure path warrants it.
9. **Mechanism remains replaceable.** Models, providers, Agent harnesses,
   visual shells, and coordination topologies are selected means rather than
   the identity of the system.
10. **Controls follow practice.** Add a recovery, isolation, approval, or
    protection mechanism when it interrupts a demonstrated material failure
    path. Do not design the protection system more completely than the
    productive loop it is meant to preserve.

## The transition from supervision

Every transitional human operation must name the autonomous ability it
temporarily supplies and an observation under which the operation can recede.

| Transitional form | Missing ability it currently supplies | Evidence that permits it to recede |
|---|---|---|
| human creates and decomposes tasks | intent interpretation and autonomous work formation | representative goals are decomposed, executed, and revised without omitted obligations or manual reconstruction |
| human assigns Agents and chooses topology | task-shape diagnosis and self-organization | the system changes formation in response to dependency evidence and improves whole-task outcomes |
| human notices stalled or drifting work | self-sensing and supervisory recovery | blocked, repetitive, or divergent loops are detected and recover or escalate without routine inspection |
| human approves ordinary reversible steps | uncertainty-aware autonomous action | the system acts, narrows, delays, or asks according to observed consequence without avoidable interruptions |
| task list is the primary interaction | natural expression and collective-state perception | a person can understand, redirect, and recover work without administering the queue |
| supervised Workbench is the operating center | sustained multi-project autonomy | projects continue useful work, survive interruption, coordinate resources, and present only decision-changing human needs |

These are evidence directions, not fixed release gates or promises that every
human role disappears. The point is to prevent a temporary limitation from
becoming a permanent architectural dependency.

## Measures of progress

Evaluate progress toward the final form through outcomes such as:

- duration of useful autonomous operation before avoidable human repair;
- share of work whose decomposition, scheduling, verification, and recovery
  occur without stepwise human administration;
- useful accepted outcomes per unit of human attention, time, and cost;
- productive capability affordable to ordinary users rather than dependent on
  enterprise-scale capital;
- recovery from Agent, process, tool, provider, and context failure without
  losing the mission;
- reduction in repeated corrections for the same class of failure;
- evidence that a changed method or organization improves later outcomes;
- ability to replace Agents, models, and interfaces without losing continuity,
  while retaining inspectable evidence and avoiding an indispensable hidden
  control point;
- practitioners' ability to control relevant data, inspect and accept results,
  exit, and retain or fork the productive capability; and
- the person's ability to express and revise intent without learning the
  internal workflow.

Do not substitute uptime, Agent count, task count, token volume, animation,
approval throughput, or self-reported confidence for these measures.

## Drift tests

Before accepting a material design or implementation, ask:

- Does it let the system sustain a useful Loop for longer, or merely describe
  more process around it?
- Does the gain make productive capability more accessible, economical,
  replaceable, and retainable under the Founding Mandate?
- Does it reduce the person's operational burden, or turn another system
  uncertainty into an approval?
- Can the relevant state survive replacement and interruption?
- What observation will cause later behavior or organization to change?
- Is a Swarm selected because the work is decomposable, or because multiple
  Agents look more autonomous?
- Is the UI projecting real state, or creating a visual fiction of activity?
- Is a fixed role or gate an enduring human responsibility, or transitional
  compensation for an immature ability?
- Is the control proportional to a demonstrated failure path, or has protection
  become the main product?
- Can an ordinary person interact from their own purpose, or must they become
  the system's project manager?

A design that repeatedly fails these tests may be locally correct and still
move Rossovia away from its goal.

## Present standing

Rossovia does not yet satisfy this program. Work Cell, Autonomy, and Workbench
provide important local mechanisms and evidence, but current formal operation
is supervised and human-initiated. The next valid transitions must be learned
from representative operation rather than inferred from this document.

This draft records the direction in which those transitions are judged:
supervision should teach the system how to need less supervision; interfaces
should move from task operation toward natural perception and conversation;
and every successful loop should make later collective work more capable of
continuing on its own.
